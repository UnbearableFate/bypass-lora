## 1. State: what the evidence supports and what it does not

**The start stage produced a usable hop-1 read at the answer position; the gate failure is a scope artifact of the criterion, not a property of the model.**

- EV-0111 (diagnostic, seed 0): hop-1 EM 89/334 = 26.6 %, zero 0, shuffled 1.2 %, margin 25.4 pp (2.5x the 10 pp gate); NLL 3.63 vs 8.18. Answer-position target weight 0.384, answer-position argmax = target in 169/334 = 50.6 % of rows (recomputed), most-common last slot 7.8 % (no fixed-slot collapse; the collapsed V2 arms were 86–100 %).
- Arithmetic of the failed clause. Per row, `all = ((T−1)·nonlast + last)/T`. Recomputed on hop 1: T = 23 (757 rows) or 25 (243 rows); all 0.066, last 0.384, non-last 0.052. With the non-last positions unchanged, even a perfect answer-position read (last = 1.0) gives an all-position mean of 0.090–0.093, below 0.125. On T1 (T = 7) the same adapter family had non-last 0.094 (from EV-0095: (7·0.162 − 0.571)/6), i.e. the non-last reads were near-uniform there too; T1 passed the clause only because one of seven positions is the answer position. The clause measures question length, not read quality. The coordinator's hope that the all-position weight "still rising 0.050 → 0.066" could reach 0.125 is therefore unfounded: it cannot.
- Audit of the revision (I went through every gated result in the record: EV-0070's ten V2 arms, E-0014 R1–R6, E-0016 D1–D5, E-0017 P1/P2/C1/C2, E-0018's three seeds): in no case does the verdict depend on the weight scope. Every arm that failed the all-position clause also failed the EM clause (R5 all 0.143/last 0.518 but EM +4.6 pp; D2a last 0.162, D2b last 0.135 but EM +3.2/+3.0 pp; D3/D4 last 0.061/0.063; all others EM at chance), and every arm that passed EM also had answer-position weight ≥ 0.5 (D5 0.571, P1 0.610, P2 0.600, C1 0.589, C2 0.598). The start stage is the first result in the project where the two scopes disagree, and the disagreement is exactly the 3.4x question-length ratio. Switching the clause to the answer position changes no recorded verdict.

**Hop 1 is now limited by two factors of similar size, address hit and decode-given-hit (recomputed, hop-1 normal records).**

- EM | argmax = target: 86/169 = 50.9 %; EM | argmax ≠ target: 3/165 = 1.8 %. So hop-1 EM ≈ 0.51 (hit) × 0.51 (decode). Among hits, EM rises with the answer-position weight: 25 % at 0.2–0.4, 46 % at 0.4–0.6, 64 % at 0.6–0.8, 60 % at 0.8–1.0; the ceiling matches oracle_value 66.8 %. The oracle_value drop 75.8 → 66.8 % is real but the per-row data says the decoder cap now costs as much hop-1 EM as the address does; both are trained further by the arms themselves (one third of arm rows are hop 1).
- The decoder is frequency-biased: hop-1 EM 77/241 = 32.0 % for high-frequency answer names vs 12/93 = 12.9 % for low-frequency names. oracle_value by target fact position is flat (61.5 / 62.1 / 71.0 % for positions 0 / 1–7 / 8–15): the isolated write removed the position dependence that broke V2.
- Where the misses go: 159/334 = 47.6 % to a same-relation fact on another chain, 6 (1.8 %) to the predecessor fact (W r X), 0 to a fact of another relation. Relation is fully discriminated; what the key/query pair separates weakly is the head entity among the 12 same-relation facts (uniform over them would be 8.3 %; the model hits 50.6 %). oracle_key confirms the key side: the target's own stored key as query gives weight only 0.175 with n_eff 8.7, i.e. the stored keys of same-relation facts are near-collinear; the learned answer-position query does better (0.384) by its own scaling. Support order, terminal distractor and presence of a predecessor fact make no difference beyond noise (22.8 vs 30.5 %, 24.6 vs 28.7 %, 27.5 vs 25.7 %; n = 167–182 per cell).
- Training state: train CE 4.74 → 2.88 (mean of steps 5500–6000) vs dev hop-1 NLL 3.63 after about 8 epochs over the 6,144 hop-1 train rows; dev EM was still rising (22.5 → 26.6 % in the last quarter, under an LR already below 8e-4). Not overfit yet, but the hop-1 train set is small and a second cycle would be 16 epochs.

**A structural fact that changes how the ten-arm matrix should be read (checked in `src/bypass_model.py`, `AdaptedLayer.forward`).** The branch's query features are taken from the layer-14 input (`n = input_layernorm(hidden)`), and the read `z` is added after the FFN of layer 14 (`F_out`: `h + mlp(u) + z@P`). A later question position's layer-14 input depends only on layers ≤ 13 of earlier positions, which never contain a read. So at S = 1 no query can depend on a previous read; every read is addressed from the question text (X, r) alone, and the only way a read can depend on a read is inside the loop (S ≥ 2). Consequences:
- V3-01 (S1), V3-05 (fixed query), V3-06 (no memory) and V3-07 (wider S1) cannot compose two hops by construction. Their hops 2–3 EM is a floor to be measured, not a competitor. (The one residual path, the frozen layers 15–27 combining reads made at different question positions, has no trained parameters beyond P and cannot target the chain; I would not assume it is exactly zero, which is why V3-01 must still be run.)
- Rule 4's clauses (c), (d), (e) therefore follow from clause (a) up to noise. The matrix's information for H-0005@2 lies in (a) S_M* − V3-01, (b) hop-1 non-regression, S2 vs S4, and the per-step trajectories; seven of the ten arms (about 1.3 of the 1.8 node·h) are controls whose primary-metric outcome is predetermined.
- S2 cannot solve hop 3 (one read per hop). Its primary metric (hop2 + hop3)/2 is capped near hop2/2, so expect S4 > S2 on hop 3 if chaining works at all; conversely S4 must handle one or two "overshoot" reads on hop-1/hop-2 rows with the increment readout (sum of all reads), so clause (b) (hop-1 drop ≤ 1 pp) is the clause most at risk for S4, and V3-09/V3-10 exist for exactly that failure.

## 2. Related research

1. Sukhbaatar, Szlam, Weston, Fergus, "End-To-End Memory Networks", NeurIPS 2015, arXiv:1503.08895, §2.2 Eq. (4), §4.2, §4.4, Table 1, App. A Table 3. **Checked: full text fetched and quoted (via a subagent); numbers below are from the paper's tables.** The hop update is `u^{k+1} = u^k + o^k`, which is the accumulate loop of plan §3.5 with η = 1; layer-wise (RNN-like) tying adds a linear map, `u^{k+1} = H u^k + o^k`, i.e. the tied-Rq/Ro form (V3-03) vs per-hop weights (adjacent tying, V3-08). Joint bAbI mean error for 1/2/3 hops: 25.8/15.6/13.3 % (1k), 24.5/10.9/7.9 % (10k); most gain is 1 → 2 hops, 2 → 3 is small and mixed. Path finding (task 19), the closest analogue of T2, stays at 73–91 % error for all hop counts except a wider ReLU variant (2.3 %). "Linear start" (softmax removed until validation loss stalls, then re-inserted) was needed to avoid local minima (task 16: 52 % → 1.6 %), and each run was restarted 10 times keeping the best. Relevance: the V3 loop is this mechanism inside a frozen LLM layer; multi-hop attention is learnable from answer supervision alone, but chain tasks are the hard case and optimization, not capacity, was the usual failure.
2. Saunshi et al., "Reasoning with Latent Thoughts: On the Power of Looped Transformers", ICLR 2025, arXiv:2502.17416, §2.1 Table 1, §5.3 Cor. 5.3. **Checked: full text fetched and quoted (via a subagent).** On p-hop induction a 1-layer block looped 6 times reaches 99.9 % (p = 16) and 99.5 % (p = 32) vs 48.9 / 49.0 % for the same block non-looped; depth, not parameter count, carries multi-hop composition. Relevance: supports the premise that a shared read block iterated S times can learn chaining from scratch; also that the loop count must at least cover the hop count.
3. Sanford, Hsu, Telgarsky, "Transformers, parallel computation, and logarithmic depth", ICML 2024, arXiv:2402.09268, §4 Thm 4.2. **Not checked beyond search summaries.** k-hop needs depth Θ(log k) in transformers; recurrent or low-rank attention needs depth Ω(k). Relevance: our loop reads one fact per step, so S ≥ hop count is the sequential regime; S4 covers hops ≤ 3, S2 covers hop 2 only.
4. Balesni, Korbak, Evans, "The Two-Hop Curse", arXiv:2411.16353. **Not checked beyond search summaries.** Latent two-hop composition succeeds when both facts are present in context and fails for separately learned parametric facts. Relevance: the memory here is a context-like store, so composition is plausible, but single-hop competence does not imply composition; the arms must learn it on hops 2–3 rows.
5. Wang et al., "Grokked Transformers are Implicit Reasoners", NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2405.15071. **Not checked beyond search summaries.** Two-hop composition circuits emerge only after training far past the point where training loss plateaus; the authors recommend memory augmentation or explicit recurrence. Relevance: 6,000 steps per arm may be short for chaining to appear; the per-step trajectory should be read before calling a null result.
6. "Identity Bridge: Enabling Implicit Reasoning via Shared Latent Memory", arXiv:2509.24653. **Not checked beyond search summaries.** Adding zero-hop (X → X) examples aligns the head-role and tail-role representations of an entity and unlocks out-of-distribution two-hop generalization in synthetic settings. Relevance: the loop's step-2 query must map the value of "X r Y" (tail role of Y) to the key of "Y r Z" (head role of Y) through R_o·R_q; this is the alignment the arms have to learn without labels.

## 4. Proposals (differ in mechanism)

**P1. Answer-position gate plus staged screening (decision structure, no model change).**
- Explains: the gate failure (scope artifact, see §1) and why the ten arms are not the cheapest route to the decision (controls at floor).
- Difference: changes the criterion's scope and the run order; the model, data, recipe and the pre-registered rules 2–9 stay as sealed.
- Criterion (E-0022@3, hop 1, S = 1, T2 dev): EM margin ≥ 10 pp; NLL clause unchanged; **answer-position (last question position) target weight ≥ 0.125**; **most-common last slot ≤ 0.25** (keeps the non-degeneracy intent, separates D5-family 0.076–0.078 from the collapsed arms 0.86–1.0). The all-position mean becomes descriptive. Label every evidence record from these arms "start criterion revised after the start result, audited against all prior gated results (no verdict changes)"; V4 pre-registers the answer-position criterion from the outset so V4 is clean. For future templates define the scope as "the position whose logits are scored".
- Stage A: V3-01, V3-02, V3-03 only, from the common start, 6,000 steps, same order. Stage B (V3-04..V3-10) only if S_M* − V3-01 ≥ +3 pp on hops 2–3 (rule 4a) and hop-1 drop ≤ 1 pp (4b); if 4a holds but 4b fails, run V3-09/V3-10 (readout variants) before the rest; if S_M* − V3-01 ≤ 0, record challenges per rule 7 with the trajectory evidence and do not run the controls; if 0 < diff < 3, describe and decide between P3 and a linear-start variant (below). Pre-register one secondary mechanism outcome, reported not gating: in hop-2 rows, S_M*'s answer-position weight on path fact 2 at step ≥ 2, plus the planned intermediate-state intervention after step 1. This distinguishes genuine chaining from a readout artifact and tells, on a null result, whether addressing or readout failed.
- Prediction: if chaining is learnable here, S4 hop-2 EM exceeds V3-01 by well over 3 pp (V3-01 hops 2–3 should sit at the prior, 1–5 %, as the start stage does: 4.2 / 0.9 %) and the step-2 weight on fact 2 is above 0.125; S2 gains on hop 2 only. Controls: V3-01 plus the per-arm zero/shuffled/permuted controls and the E-0011 shortcut baselines.
- Cheapest test and cost (T-0021 timings, 2g): V3-01 0.09, V3-02 0.10, V3-03 0.14 node·h training plus about 0.04 each for evaluation and the S_eval scan: about 0.44 node·h, roughly 45 min wall with the three jobs in parallel. Stage B adds about 1.3 node·h only when there is a gain to explain, so the full positive path costs what the contract already budgets and the negative path saves about 1.3 node·h.
- Drop if: S2 and S4 both ≤ V3-01 on hops 2–3 and step-2 weight on fact 2 < 1/16 with hop-1 hit rate unchanged: then the loop does not re-address from this start, and the next money goes to P2/P3 or to the MemN2N linear-start variant (softmax temperature annealed from flat for the first part of training, one S4 arm, about 0.14 node·h), not to more controls.

**P2. Second start cycle (optimization budget on the hop-1 address; same recipe).**
- Explains: dev EM was still rising at the end of a decayed cosine cycle (22.5 → 26.6 % in the last quarter); address learning was the slow component in every arm in this project and in MemN2N.
- Difference from the arms: hop-1 rows only, warm restart (AdamW reset, warmup 300, cosine, 6,000 steps) from ATT-0196-1; from P1: it changes the start, not the gate.
- Prediction: hop-1 hit rate rises from 50.6 % toward the T1 level (T1 answer-position weight 0.571, EM 42.8 %); EM margin ≥ 30 pp; oracle_value may erode a few more points, which the arms repair (hop-1 rows). Controls: the same six controls; the pre-registered choice rule "use start-2 iff it passes the P1 gate and its hop-1 EM margin ≥ start-1's; otherwise start-1" removes post-hoc picking.
- Cheapest test and cost: one job, about 0.09 node·h, about 25 min wall. Run it before Stage A (serial cost 25 min) so that a null Stage A is not confounded by a 50 % hop-1 hit rate; do not rerun Stage A twice.
- Drop if: hit rate or EM margin do not improve over start-1 (then the head-entity separation is representational, and only P3-type changes or the arms' own training can move it; note 16 epochs over 6,144 rows is the overfitting risk to watch via train CE vs dev NLL).

**P3. Head-token key (write-side mechanism for same-relation head discrimination).**
- Explains: 47.6 % of answer-position misses land on same-relation facts with a different head and 0 % on other relations; oracle_key weight 0.175 (n_eff 8.7) shows the stored keys of same-relation facts are near-collinear. In the isolated encoding "X r Y ;" the key is taken at the delimiter, whose layer-14 state is dominated by the nearest tokens (r, Y); the head X is the weakest component, yet it is what hop-1 and every chaining step must match.
- Difference from routes tried: all V2/E-0016/E-0017 routes changed training (curricula, prior path, address supervision, isolated write) while keeping key = value position = delimiter. P3 keeps the value at the delimiter (so the decoder and H-0012's result are untouched) and takes the key from the head-entity token's state (first token of the isolated fact), or concatenates head-token and delimiter features before A_k. It is a declared change of the write protocol (new registered variant, statistics reported separately), not a change of the sealed data.
- Prediction and controls: hop-1 hit rate well above 70 % and oracle_key weight well above 0.175 with head-token keys; a tail-token key control should not help hop 1 (it would make the predecessor fact W r X compete) and the delimiter key is the baseline. If the loop then chains, the step-2 query must match a head-token key from a delimiter-derived value, which the trajectories will show.
- Cheapest test and cost: code plus review, then one start-stage job (about 0.09 node·h) with the standard evaluation; about 0.15 node·h all in.
- Drop if: hit rate does not exceed start-1's 50.6 % by at least 10 pp, or hop-1 EM margin falls (decoder disturbed).

## 5. Recommendation: continue (P1 now, P2 before Stage A, P3 held as the contingency)

Direct answers to the three questions:
- **Most decision-relevant evidence per node·h:** P2 (0.09) followed by Stage A of P1 (about 0.44) from the better start. Three arms give the yes/no on chaining; the seven controls add nothing to the hops 2–3 verdict until there is a gain to explain, and then they cost what the contract already budgets. Changing the template or the start recipe (option c) is not supported by the data: relation is fully discriminated and the answer-position read works at 0.384; a shorter template would raise the all-position mean mechanically, not the address. Stopping (option d) is not justified: the remaining cost is about 0.5 node·h for an interpretable answer either way.
- **Pre-registered start criterion for T2:** EM margin ≥ 10 pp, NLL clause, answer-position target weight ≥ 0.125, most-common last slot ≤ 0.25 (hop 1, S = 1); all-position weight descriptive. Carry the "revised after result, audited" label on the V3 evidence; pre-register it cleanly in V4.
- **Is a hop-1-only start a valid base for hops 2–3?** Yes, and it is the only sensible common start: every arm shares it (plan §9.1 fairness); chaining cannot be learned at S = 1 (structural, §1), so it must be learned inside the arms on hops 1–3 rows, exactly as MemN2N learns multi-hop attention from answer supervision; a hops 1–3 start at S = 1 would train the decoder to hedge toward unreachable answers, and a hops 1–3 start at S > 1 is already an arm. The start only has to make the first read usable; the arms' trajectories (step-2 weight on fact 2, intervention after step 1) are the evidence that the second read exists.

Risks the coordinator should pre-decide: (1) S4 may gain on hops 2–3 and lose more than 1 pp on hop 1 through overshoot reads under the increment readout; decide now that this triggers V3-09/V3-10 rather than a pause. (2) A single-seed null in 6,000 steps may be an optimization failure (MemN2N needed linear start and restarts; grokking-type composition is slow); decide now that a null with no step-2 re-addressing buys one linear-start S4 arm (about 0.14 node·h) before H-0005 is paused. (3) The decoder's frequency bias (32 % vs 13 % hop-1 EM) will also cap hops 2–3; report EM by answer frequency stratum in every arm so a gain is not masked by low-frequency chain ends.
