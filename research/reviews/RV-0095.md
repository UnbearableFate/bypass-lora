# RV-0095 — up-only LoRA portable initialization at dc82cfc

**Verdict: pass, no findings.**

## Root cause and fix

The failure the fix targets is real. In the f46a13d calibration runs:

| Run | LR | MIG slice | `init.parameters.sha256` |
|---|---|---|---|
| ATT-0349-1 | 1e-4 | 2g.24gb | `4b9e9951…` |
| ATT-0350-1 | 3e-4 | 2g.24gb | `4b9e9951…` |
| ATT-0351-1 | 1e-3 | 1g.24gb | `65ccff56…` |

The 1g run's start differs from the two 2g runs. The fix removes the cause: `UpLoRALinear` draws A on CPU and then copies it to the device (`src/v1_lora.py:119-137`).

## Contract preservation

**Initial distribution.**
- The draw is `kaiming_uniform_(a=√5)` on a CPU FP32 tensor of shape (r, in_features), which is identical to the legacy call (:45).
- fan_in = 1024 and bound = 1/√1024, i.e. contract E-0029.md:59, "A … 按 fan-in 随机" (A drawn at random by fan-in).
- B is FP32 zeros on the device, and scale = α/r = 1 (`LORA_RANKS`, alpha = rank).

**Bitwise relation to the legacy draw.**
- `torch.empty` and `torch.zeros` consume no RNG.
- So on a CPU base the new draw is bitwise the legacy one.
- On CUDA it now equals that CPU stream.
- PyTorch's CPU `uniform_` runs as a serial kernel, so `set_num_threads(4)` does not change the values.

**Seeding.**
- `make_model` (`rb_train.py:89-96`) is unchanged.
- It calls `torch.manual_seed(derive_seed(20261101, "residual_la_v21", phase, "init", 0))` after `load_base` and immediately before wrapping.
- The CPU generator is therefore seeded per phase: V0 for calibration, R1 for train, I-G and smoke.

**Structure.**
- Registration order is unchanged: base, then A, then B. State names are unchanged.
- The target key is still `(index, 'mlp.up_proj')`.
- Counts are 4·r·(1024+3072): 917,504 and 786,432.
- `forward`, `adapter_parameters`, `export_adapter` and `load_adapter` are inherited unchanged. `load_adapter` builds the wrapper with `cls`, then copies the saved A and B.

**Legacy code.**
- The diff only adds code: `LoRALinear` and `LoRAModel` are byte-identical.
- Every legacy user (`v5_train`, `rb5_cost`, `rb_width_check`, `rb1_latency`, and RB1-07 via `rb_train:95`) still constructs `LoRAModel` with its CUDA draw.

## No other fresh E-0029 initialization draws on CUDA

**By code.**
- The side-branch A_q, A_k and A_v are drawn with `torch.randn` with no device argument (`bypass_model.py:79-81`). The P/C exit matrices are zeros (:120-124).
- `SideBranch` is built on CPU and then moved with `.to(device)` (:224).
- Rq and Ro start as identity (`bypass_core.py:242-243`).
- P pairing (`pair_qk`) is deterministic.
- No `set_default_device`, `set_default_tensor_type` or `set_default_dtype` call exists anywhere in `src`.

**By artifacts.** I compared G-QN ATT-0347-1 (f46a13d, 2g slice) with ATT-0355-1 (dc82cfc, 1g slice):
- 20 of the 24 adapter tensors are bitwise equal: A_q, A_k, Rq, Ro, C_u and the rest.
- Only the four gradient-derived `projection.A_v` differ.
- G-N ATT-0348-1 and ATT-0356-1 (both on 2g) are bitwise identical.
- So the random parts are portable across slices, and the training-time `init_check` (only A_v may differ from the random reference) holds across slices.

## Regression test

`src/test_rb_improve.py:219-237` seeds identically and builds the model on a CPU base and on a CUDA base. It then requires:
- equal `initial_state` digests;
- bitwise-equal A per target;
- all-zero B.

Under f46a13d the CUDA base drew from the CUDA generator, so this test fails there. ATT-0354-1 reports 126 passed and nothing skipped (124 + 2 new). The CUDA-gated parametrizations therefore ran.

## Observation, not a defect

The I-G A_v (data-derived on GPU, not random) is not bitwise reproducible across MIG slices. Between 2g and 1g for G-QN:
- one column has its sign flipped in each of layers 6, 13 and 20;
- otherwise |cos| ≥ 0.995 per column, and principal-angle cosines are ≥ 0.9978;
- the leading 32 singular values agree within 0.53%.

A column sign flip is an exact training symmetry here. Values enter only linearly through KernelMatrix, Ro = I and C_u = 0, and AdamW and global-norm clipping are sign-equivariant.

The formal G arms load the stored, manifest-bound I-G checkpoint (`rb_improve_train.py:141-153`; analysis :170-173), so this does not affect validity. Do not expect a later re-collection on another slice to reproduce these bytes.
