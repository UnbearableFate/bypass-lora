## 1. Assessment: what the evidence supports, at which level, and what it does not

All RB results are screening level: seed 0 (segmented line: seeds 0-2 on dev, 3-5 on A2), 1,000 dev rows per task, paired bootstrap intervals that exclude seed and selection uncertainty. Within that scope the records support five statements.

**(a) The history contrast in the ordinary setting is about zero in four matched pairs.** S1 minus its same-parameter, same-initialization T0: RB1-02 minus RB1-01 = -0.20 pp (r32, R_res, QK tap); W-02 minus W-01 = +0.15 (r128, R_res, QK tap); X-01 minus X-02 = -0.15 (r32, C_up, X tap); RB1-09 minus X-03 = -0.65 (r32, C_up, QK tap). Mean -0.21 pp, largest +0.15. Each interval is about +/-1.1 pp, but a true +1 pp history effect would have to be hidden in all four pairs across two exits, two taps and two widths. That is the strongest statement this protocol can make at the available resolution, and it is negative. The swap, permute and window drops do not contradict it (AD-0006 section 1; E-0024): they show that the trained forward depends on the state, not that the state carries information the frozen attention lacks.

**(b) What the trained X-01 read actually is** (E-0029 final read diagnostics, `runs/ATT-0373-1/analysis.json`, `read_diagnostics.X-01.final`, answer-prediction scope). At layers 6 and 13 the read is a near-uniform prefix mean: BoolQ prefixes of 129-512 tokens give n_eff 78.0 and 136.5, PIQA prefixes of 9-32 tokens give n_eff 15.8 and 14.5, and the maximum weight is at most 0.14 for prefixes of nine or more tokens. At layer 20 (PIQA) and layer 27 (both tasks) it is a one-position read: maximum weight 0.44-0.95, n_eff 1.1-5.7. RB1-09 shows the same pattern, and for RB1-09 (and RB1-08) E-0024 found that read sitting on prompt position 0, the same token in every row of a task ('Goal' / 'Pass'), with 0.37-0.75 of the layer-27 weight (0.81-0.98 in the concentrated cells of the R_res arms; E-0024's preregistered sink criterion failed only on its cross-episode constancy index, EV-0122). X-01 was trained after E-0024 and has no position-0 measurement of its own. The current token is not needed (iv_strict +0.35), the last eight tokens recover most of the state's value (iv_window8 -1.2), and another episode's past costs 2.0. The state supplies a bag-of-tokens mean plus a per-task constant; both are functions a tokenwise branch or the frozen attention's broad heads can supply. Nothing in the read is retrieval, which is why (a) holds.

**(c) The gap to all-7 LoRA r7 is an interface effect, not a history effect.** Every adapter confined to the up_proj input lands in one band: up-only LoRA r56 and r48 (917,504 and 786,432 parameters) 77.60 and 77.45; side T0 at up (X-02, 794,624) 78.30; side S1 at up (X-01) 78.15. The all-7 LoRA r7 (630,784) is 78.95. Paired intervals from ATT-0373-1: RB1-07 minus L-56 = +1.35 [+0.10, +2.60]; RB1-07 minus L-48 = +1.50 [+0.20, +2.75]; RB1-07 minus X-01 = +0.80 [-0.35, +1.95]; X-01 minus L-48 = +0.70 [-0.65, +2.05]. So the side branch is the best adapter at its interface, at fewer parameters than the up-only LoRA, but the interface itself trails an adapter spread over q, k, v, o, gate, up and down. This agrees with LoRA section 7.1 Table 5 (a budget spread over more matrices beats the same budget in one matrix), QLoRA (all linear layers needed to match full finetuning) and the project's own confirmatory E-0013 (tokenwise C_up versus 28-layer all-7 rank-1 LoRA: -0.42 pp [-1.02, +0.20], 3 seeds, official validation). Closing the 0.8 pp gap means adding insertion points. The history read will not close it (a), and the matched-parameter clause cannot be met by a 795k-parameter branch against a 631k LoRA anyway; it can only be met by comparing with a LoRA of the same count (r10 = 901,120 for a 925,696 branch).

**(d) The five options the coordinator listed, ranked by what the records already say.**
1. Writing into the attention path or more modules: the only option that addresses the gap; the mechanism is insertion points; it says nothing about history unless a T0 twin runs beside it (proposal 2).
2. Stacking on LoRA r7: tests complementarity, but (a) predicts a null, and the budget is 2.3 times LoRA r7 (1,425,408 parameters, matched LoRA r16 = 1,441,792). Run it only if an ordinary-setting positive at any parameter cost is wanted: arms LoRA7+S1(X), LoRA7+T0(X), LoRA r16; about 0.9 node-h; drop if LoRA7+S1 minus LoRA7+T0 < +1.
3. Longer training or a calibrated LR: E-0013 at 3 epochs and 3 seeds keeps the ordering; the 75-100% segment of every dev curve adds +0.2 to +0.7 pp under cosine (X-01 77.65 to 78.15, X-02 77.60 to 78.30, L-56 77.40 to 77.60, RB1-07 78.45 to 78.95); the six-point eta grid, which under Adam scales the output path much like an LR, was flat (X: 77.45-78.15). A three-point LR calibration for the X tap costs 0.15 node-h and I expect at most 0.5 pp; an optional add-on, not a route.
4. Larger rank or more layers: saturated on this dev for both families (r128 minus r32: -0.15 and -0.50; LoRA r31 minus r7: -0.20; V1 LoRA28 79.05 versus LoRA4 79.2).
5. Selective or gated reads: in the ordinary setting the frozen attention already is the selective read; the M line's read-operator comparison was inconclusive (H-0004: SoftmaxSlot better in 0 of 4 cells, KernelMatrix in 0 of 4, EV-0071); a learned gate converges to whatever T0 already does. Not worth an ordinary-setting arm; a candidate in the segmented protocol after the write-scope question (proposal 3).

**(e) Where the mechanism is measurable.** Same operator, segmented protocol: Keep minus max(Reset, T0) on BoolQ is +4.4, +2.6, +1.8 on dev (seeds 0-2) and +3.2, +1.0, +1.9 on A2 (seeds 3-5): six of six positive, mean +2.5 pp; Keep minus segmented LoRA is +1.7 to +4.4 at every seed; the swap drop is 4.9-7.0 pp at all six seeds. The line is paused (D-0075) only because +2.93 and +2.03 missed a +3 gate. None of the user's improvement ideas (X tap, C_up exit, initialization, write scope) has been tried there.

**(f) What the records do not support:** that the side history is useful on full-context PIQA/BoolQ; that it is useless in general; that r32 lacks capacity in the ordinary setting (the state is unused, not full); any ordering of arms closer than about 2 pp on one seed.

## 3. Bottleneck

The ordinary-setting improvement question is blocked by resolution, not by a shortage of variants. Thirty trained arms in this setting, including both eta grids, land between 76.80 and 78.95 (a 2.15 pp band), the paired half-width at 1,000 rows per task is 1.1 pp, and the history contrast has been within 0.65 pp of zero in four matched pairs. Any further single-seed arm in this band, including the five options listed, will return "descriptive, 0 < d < 1" with high probability whatever the truth, and a +1 pp gate cannot be passed except by luck. The decision the project needs is not "which variant next" but "in which protocol is the mechanism measurable", and the records already answer it: the same operator gives a six-for-six positive history gain of about +2.5 pp when the backbone cannot see segment 1, and about 0 when it can. The user's improvement ideas have only been tested in the protocol where nothing can be measured.

## 4. Proposals (three, different mechanisms)

### Proposal 1. Resolution upgrade with zero training: re-score the existing checkpoints on dev plus calibration rows

1. Explains: why every ordinary-setting contrast is "inconclusive": the measurement, not the models, is the limit.
2. Difference from routes tried: no new arm. Evaluate the retained final checkpoints of X-02, X-03, RB1-09, RB1-07, L-56 and L-48 on the 2,048 calibration rows per task (X-01, `runs/ATT-0321-1/calibration-predictions.json`, and the E-0028 eta arms already have them; the RB1-09 run directory does not), pool with dev to 3,048 rows per task, and recompute the paired task-stratified bootstrap (same seed and index scheme) for the contrasts X-01 minus X-02, RB1-09 minus X-03, RB1-07 minus X-01, RB1-07 minus X-02, X-01 minus L-48, X-02 minus L-48; report answer NLL beside accuracy as a continuous secondary.
3. Prediction and controls: the half-width shrinks from about 1.1 to about 0.6 pp. Under (a), both history contrasts stay inside [-0.6, +0.6]; under the coordinator's alternative, at least one is at or above +1 with a lower bound above 0. Caveat to register: calibration rows selected the family LRs and the eta values (QK winner 0.25 = RB1-09's eta; X winner 1.0, not X-01's 0.25, so the rows do not favour X-01). Twins share eta and LR, so the S1-minus-T0 contrasts are neutral; cross-family contrasts carry at most a small selection bias, and it cannot manufacture a history gain.
4. Cheapest test: six calibration evaluations (X-02, X-03, RB1-09, RB1-07, L-56, L-48) at about 0.025 node-h each, plus one analysis job. Optional: one more seed of the X-01/X-02 pair (0.6 node-h) if a 2-seed paired estimate is wanted for the stage report.
5. Expected cost: 0.15 node-h (0.75 with the optional seed).
6. Drop if: nothing to drop; if a history contrast comes out at or above +1 with a positive lower bound, proposal 2 should run with two seeds instead of one, and the ordinary-setting conclusion in the stage report is withheld until then.

### Proposal 2. Insertion-point pair in the ordinary setting: write the same Z into the attention residual as well as up_proj, with a T0 twin and a parameter-matched LoRA

1. Explains: the 0.8-1.5 pp gap to all-7 LoRA r7 shared by every up-interface adapter (c); it tests whether the gap is coverage of the attention path.
2. Difference from routes tried: not a tap, eta, init, width or loop change (so not a rule-10 tap variant). Keep X-01 exactly (direct_hidden tap, r32, KernelMatrix S1, C_up, eta 0.25, LR 1e-3, RB1 init and order) and add a second zero-initialized exit P_h in R^(32x1024) on the attention residual, i.e. the existing `F_attnres` fusion combined with `C_up`: h' = h + Z P_h, y = h' + MLP(norm(h') + Z C_u on the up input). The residual write reaches the FFN gate and every later layer, which is what LoRA on o_proj/v_proj gives. Parameters 925,696 (the RB1-09 count); matched all-7 LoRA r10 = 901,120. Twin arm: the same structure with T0 reads (X-02 plus P_h).
3. Distinguishing prediction and controls: under the insertion-point reading (mine), S1+P_h and T0+P_h both gain +0.5 to +1.0 over X-01/X-02 and land within +/-0.5 of RB1-07, with S1+P_h minus T0+P_h about 0. Under "history adds information at the attention residual", S1+P_h minus T0+P_h is at least +1, which would be the first such result in this project. Controls: X-01 and X-02 (same initial A matrices, element-wise), RB1-07, LoRA r10 (optional, for the matched-parameter clause), iv_off = Base, step-0 = Base, reload identity. Preregistered rules: d_ins(S1) = S1+P_h minus X-01, d_ins(T0) = T0+P_h minus X-02, d_hist = S1+P_h minus T0+P_h, d_L = S1+P_h minus RB1-07 (and minus LoRA r10); d_hist at or above +1 -> two new seeds; d_hist at or below 0 -> record "no history contribution at either exit"; d_ins at or above +1 for both twins -> the gap is insertion points, the T0+P_h twin is the practical adapter at that budget.
4. Cheapest test: two training arms (0.3 node-h each), interventions on the S1 arm (0.13), optional LoRA r10 (0.3), smoke, review and locked analysis (0.2); one implementation task (a combined fusion kind, a few dozen lines, tests for zero-exit identity and parameter counts).
5. Expected cost: 0.9-1.3 node-h. Hard cap 1.5 node-h, and the end of ordinary-setting improvement work whatever the outcome, unless d_hist fires.
6. Drop if: max(d_ins) < +1 (insertion points at r32 do not close the gap; what remains is the LoRA's change of the attention routing itself, which no bypass can supply), or d_hist at or below 0 (expected). Either outcome closes the ordinary-setting route with a mechanistic statement rather than another "inconclusive".

Rule 10 handling for proposals 1 and 2: proposal 1 trains nothing. Proposal 2 keeps the tap and source fixed at the E-0028 selection and changes only the exit, so it is outside rule 10's wording ("no more tap variants") and outside D-0065's boundary ("no new tap or source contract"). The coordinator should still record one route decision that (i) cites the user's 2026-11-03 request as the "user asks otherwise" reopen case named in D-0066, (ii) states that rule 10 stays in force for tap and source, (iii) preregisters the rules above with a 1.5 node-h cap, and (iv) declares that ordinary-setting improvement work ends after proposal 2 unless d_hist fires.

### Proposal 3. Take the improvement screen to the protocol where the mechanism is measurable: user's variants in the segmented protocol with the Keep/Reset/T0 triple

1. Explains: (a) and (e) together: the state is used only when the backbone cannot see the information, so an improvement of the read, the write or the value source can only register where the gain is nonzero, and there the gain is +2.5 pp with a 4.9-7.0 pp swap drop, enough dynamic range for a +1 to +2 pp improvement to show on three seeds.
2. Difference from routes tried: E-0025/E-0027/E-0030 ran one configuration (QK tap, R_res, all-token writes, r32/r128). The variants are the user's own ideas plus one mechanism-different axis, each changing one thing from SEG-05 (r128 Keep): (i) write scope in segment 1: last-token-only write (one write; a one-slot summary, which tests whether addressing over the passage matters at all) and sentence-final-token writes (5-10 writes; Activation Beacon style, reduces additive interference in the 128x128 state); these need no new Reset/T0 twins because segment 2 is unchanged, so SEG-06/SEG-07 of the same seed are their controls; (ii) the X tap (direct_hidden) for q, k and v, with its own Reset and T0 twins (parameters 2,228,224 versus 2,752,512); (iii) optional, last: r256 (state 1.05 MB versus 264 KB; 5,767,168 parameters) with twins. The C_up exit and the I-G initialization are second-tier variants, to be added only if (i) or (ii) moves the gain.
3. Distinguishing prediction and controls: primary BoolQ (the task with the larger measured gain), PIQA secondary with a no-regression clause. Seed-0 screen per variant V: Keep_V(0) minus Keep_ref(0) = 65.9 at or above +1.5 pp and gain_V(0) = Keep_V minus max(Reset_V, T0_V) at or above +3 -> release seeds 1 and 2 for V. Final rule, identical to E-0027 rule 4 so that a pass is the registered reopen of the segmented line: three-seed mean gain G_V at or above +3, L_V = mean(Keep_V minus LoRA_seg) at or above +1, mean swap drop at or above G_V/2, PIQA Keep_V minus max control not more than 1 pp below the reference at any seed. Controls: Reset (same operator, state cleared at the boundary), T0 (same parameters, no history), LoRA_seg r7, iv_off = Base_seg, iv_swap, write and read counts, state bytes. Prediction if the last-token write matches all-token Keep: the state acts as a summary vector and addressing over passage tokens is unused; sentence-final writes should then beat all-token writes because they put the same summaries into fewer, cleaner slots. Prediction for the X tap: a larger BoolQ gain if value content, not addressing, limits the read (E-0012's probes decoded the target from direct_hidden features more often than from qknorm features: delimiter-tail 32,648 vs 30,433 of 32,768, supervised address 1,804-1,847 vs 1,684-1,756 of 2,048, EV-0072, descriptive only).
4. Cheapest test: seed 0 first: two write-scope Keep arms (2 x 0.33 node-h) and the X-tap triple (3 x 0.33), smoke/review 0.2, about 1.9 node-h; r256 triple adds about 1.2. Replicate only the best variant at seeds 1 and 2 (1 or 3 arms per seed, 0.7-2.0 node-h). Implementation: `seg_model.py` builds arms with `adapter_config('RB1-02')` plus `replace(rank=128)`, so the tap and width variants are config replacements; the write-scope variants need a segment-1 write mask passed to `process_prefix(write=...)` (already supported in `bypass_core`) and a tokenizer-based sentence-end rule registered before training.
5. Expected cost: 2.5-4.5 node-h total (upper figure with r256), within the 165 node-h remaining. A pass triggers D-0075's reopen condition and then needs the user's decision on a new sealed confirmation set, sized from the observed effect (about +2.5 pp with per-seed spread 1.3 pp: at least 3 seeds and 2,000 rows per task).
6. Drop if: no variant clears the seed-0 screen, or the replicated G_V stays below +3 with no variant at least +1 above the E-0027 mean gain. Then the user's improvement ideas do not help the mechanism where it is measurable; keep the +2 to +3 pp segmented effect as an unconfirmed positive, write the stage report, and stop the side-branch program's improvement route.

## 5. Recommendation

Continue, with the work redirected. In the ordinary setting, spend at most 1.5 node-h: proposal 1 to put a 0.6 pp interval on the history null and the LoRA gap at zero training cost, and proposal 2 once, which either closes the gap with a T0-equivalent adapter (showing the gap is insertion points) or produces the first positive history contrast; then stop ordinary-setting improvement work regardless, because (a), (b) and (c) already say the branch is a tokenwise adapter plus a prefix mean there, and Card et al. say the test set cannot order the remaining differences. Put the user's "improve the method" effort into proposal 3: it is the only setting in which the X tap, the write scope and the width can change a measured quantity, its success rule is the one that already governs the paused segmented line, and a pass is the registered way to reopen it. Do not run stacking on LoRA r7 or selective reads in the ordinary setting; do not widen or add layers there. A stop of the whole side-branch program is not warranted while six of six segmented seeds are positive; a stop of the ordinary-setting improvement route after proposal 2 is.
